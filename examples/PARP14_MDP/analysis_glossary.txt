PARP14 CALVADOS Analysis — Glossary of Metrics
================================================

This file describes each analysis module in analyze_all.py,
what it computes, what it means physically, and what figures it produces.


1. CONFORMATIONAL PROPERTIES (Rg, Ree)
---------------------------------------

Radius of Gyration (Rg):
  Mass-weighted root-mean-square distance of all residues from the
  chain's center of mass. Measures how compact or extended the protein
  is overall. A smaller Rg means a more compact, globular conformation;
  a larger Rg means a more extended or unfolded chain.

  Computed per frame using CALVADOS calc_rg, which weights each residue
  (one bead = one CA) by its amino acid molecular weight from the
  residues CSV file.

  Units: nm

End-to-End Distance (Ree):
  Euclidean distance between the first and last residue (N-terminus to
  C-terminus) of the chain. For a fully compact globular protein Ree is
  much smaller than the contour length; for an extended or disordered
  chain Ree approaches the contour length. Together with Rg, Ree
  characterizes the overall shape: the ratio Ree/Rg distinguishes
  rod-like (~sqrt(12)) from spherical (~sqrt(5)) conformations.

  Units: nm

Figures:
  - rg_timeseries.{png,svg}   : Rg vs time for each construct (all 25
    replicates overlaid, mean +/- SD shaded)
  - rg_convergence.{png,svg}  : Cumulative running-mean Rg to check
    equilibration
  - conf_prop_distributions.{png,svg} : Violin + jitter of per-replicate
    mean Rg and Ree across constructs


2. DISTANCE MAPS (DMAP)
------------------------

Interdomain COM Distances:
  For each pair of structural domains in a construct, computes the
  Euclidean distance between domain centers of mass (COM) at every
  frame after equilibration. The mean distance map shows which domains
  are close together (strongly interacting) and which are far apart.
  The standard deviation map shows which domain pairs have the most
  variable separation, indicating flexible or dynamic interfaces.

  Only domains identified by the FL restraint boundaries are tracked
  (RRM1-3, KH1-6, KH7a, MD1, MD2, MD3, KHb-KH8, WWE, ART — whichever
  are present in the construct).

  Units: nm

Figures:
  - {set}_dmap_mean.{png,svg} : Mean interdomain COM distance matrix
  - {set}_dmap_std.{png,svg}  : Standard deviation of interdomain distances


3. CONTACT MAPS (CMAP)
-----------------------

Interdomain Contact Frequency:
  A contact between two residues is defined as their CA-CA distance
  being below a cutoff (typically 0.8 nm, from CALVADOS calc_cmap).
  The contact map is accumulated over all equilibrated frames using
  cmap_traj, giving the fraction of frames in which each residue pair
  is in contact.

  The interdomain contact frequency matrix collapses the per-residue
  contact map into a per-domain summary: for each domain pair, the
  total number of inter-domain contacts normalized by the product of
  domain sizes. High values indicate a persistent structural interface
  between two domains; low values indicate domains that rarely touch.

  Units: fractional frequency (0 to 1)

Figures:
  - {set}_cmap.{png,svg}    : Per-residue contact frequency heatmap
  - {set}_fnc.{png,svg}     : Interdomain contact frequency matrix


4. FRACTION OF NATIVE CONTACTS (FNC)
--------------------------------------

  For each structured domain, computes what fraction of the contacts
  present in the input (AF2/AF3) reference structure are maintained
  during the simulation. Uses CALVADOS calc_fnc, which compares the
  contact map at each frame to the initial reference.

  FNC = 1.0 means the domain perfectly retains its native fold. FNC
  close to 0 means the domain has lost most of its internal contacts
  (unfolded). Since CALVADOS uses harmonic restraints within structured
  domains, FNC should remain high (~0.8-1.0) for restrained regions
  and may be lower for flexible linkers.

  Units: fraction (0 to 1)

Figures:
  - {set}_fnc_timeseries.{png,svg} : FNC vs time per domain


5. ENERGY DECOMPOSITION
-------------------------

  Computes the pairwise non-bonded interaction energy between every
  pair of structural domains, decomposed into two components:

  Ashbaugh-Hatch (AH) Energy:
    Hydrophobic interaction potential. A modified Lennard-Jones potential
    where the attractive well depth is scaled by a residue-pair-specific
    hydrophobicity parameter lambda (0 = fully repulsive/hydrophilic,
    1 = full LJ attraction/hydrophobic). The sigma and lambda for each
    residue are taken from the CALVADOS force field parametrization
    (residues_CALVADOS3.csv). The potential has three distance regimes:
      - r < 2^(1/6)*sigma : repulsive core (always present)
      - 2^(1/6)*sigma < r < cutoff_lj : attractive well scaled by lambda
      - r > cutoff_lj : zero (cutoff at 2.0 nm)
    Negative AH energy means net hydrophobic attraction between domains.

    Reference: Ashbaugh & Hatch, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 130, 9536 (2008);
    CALVADOS parametrization: Tesei et al., PNAS 118, e2111696118 (2021).

  Yukawa (Debye-Huckel) Energy:
    Screened electrostatic interaction. Each residue carries a formal
    charge at the simulation pH (from the residues CSV), and the
    pairwise Coulomb interaction is screened by the solvent via a
    Yukawa (exponentially decaying) potential:
      U(r) = q_i * q_j * prefactor * exp(-kappa*r) / r
    where kappa is the inverse Debye screening length computed from
    the ionic strength (0.19 M) and temperature (293 K), and the
    prefactor includes the Bjerrum length (temperature-dependent
    dielectric constant of water). Cutoff at 4.0 nm.
    Negative Yukawa energy = attractive (opposite charges);
    positive = repulsive (like charges).

    Reference: Debye-Huckel theory; CALVADOS implementation follows
    Dignon et al., PLoS Comput. Biol. 14, e1005941 (2018).

  The total energy is the sum of AH + Yukawa per domain pair.
  Computed from a single representative replicate (seed-1, sample-0),
  sampled at 10 evenly spaced frames across the equilibrated trajectory.

  Domain boundaries for energy analysis are trimmed by 3 residues at
  each side where adjacent domains have zero or 1-residue gaps. This
  prevents steric-clash artifacts from near-boundary residue pairs
  that sit inside the AH repulsive core (~0.5 nm) due to chain
  connectivity rather than meaningful interdomain interaction. The
  trimmed boundaries are:

    Domain      Original       Trimmed        Change
    ------      --------       -------        ------
    RRM1          6 -   88       6 -   88     (no change)
    RRM2        150 -  223     150 -  223     (no change)
    RRM3        227 -  301     227 -  301     (no change)
    KH1-6       315 -  737     315 -  734     end -3 (gap 0 to KH7a)
    KH7a        738 -  789     741 -  786     both -3
    MD1         791 -  978     794 -  978     start +3 (gap 1 from KH7a)
    MD2        1003 - 1190    1003 - 1190     (no change)
    MD3        1216 - 1387    1216 - 1384     end -3 (gap 1 to KHb-KH8)
    KHb-KH8    1389 - 1533    1392 - 1530     both -3
    WWE        1534 - 1601    1537 - 1601     start +3 (gap 0 from KHb)
    ART        1605 - 1801    1605 - 1801     (no change)

  This trimming removes at most 6 residues per domain (<2% of each),
  all at linker-like boundaries, with negligible effect on distant
  domain-pair energies.

  Units: kJ/mol

  Typical value ranges:
    -5 to -11 kJ/mol : Strong attraction (tight persistent interface,
                        e.g. MD1-MD2 at -8 to -11 kJ/mol in smaller
                        constructs where macrodomains pack together)
    -1 to  -5 kJ/mol : Moderate attraction (frequent contacts, e.g.
                        MD1-ART at -3 to -5 kJ/mol)
    -0.1 to -1 kJ/mol: Weak/transient attraction (e.g. distant domains
                        like RRM1-KH1-6 at -0.3 kJ/mol)
    ~0 kJ/mol        : No significant interaction (domains too far apart
                        to contact, e.g. RRM1-ART)
    Positive          : Rare after boundary trimming. May occur for
                        domain pairs that are still very tightly packed
                        in a specific structure (e.g. FL KHb-KH8-WWE)

  Colorbar: Fixed at +/- 12 kJ/mol across all constructs for direct
  comparison. This range encompasses the full data (strongest
  attraction MD1-MD2 ~ -11 kJ/mol, residual boundary steric
  repulsion FL KHb-KH8-WWE ~ +12 kJ/mol) so no values are clipped.

  All plots use the canonical 11-domain grid (RRM1 through ART)
  regardless of construct. Domains absent from a construct are shown
  as gray cells, enabling direct visual comparison across constructs.

Figures:
  - {set}_energy.{png,svg}        : Three-panel heatmap (AH, Yukawa,
    Total) on the canonical 11-domain grid. Gray = domain not in
    construct. Blue = attractive, red = repulsive. Scale: +/- 1 kJ/mol.
  - energy_diff_vs_fl.{png,svg}   : Each construct minus FL. Shows how
    removing domains changes the remaining interdomain energies.
    Blue = more attractive in construct, red = more repulsive.
  - energy_all_vs_all.{png,svg}   : NxN grid. Diagonal = absolute
    energy for each construct. Lower triangle = row minus column
    (pairwise differences between all constructs).


6. WEIGHTED COORDINATION NUMBER (WCN)
---------------------------------------

  Measures how buried or exposed each active site is within the
  protein chain, using a smooth distance-dependent count of
  neighboring residues. For a given active site (defined by its
  catalytic residue positions), WCN sums over all other residues
  using a switching function:

    WCN = sum_j [ (1 - (r_ij/r0)^6) / (1 - (r_ij/r0)^12) ]

  where r_ij is the distance from the active site COM to residue j,
  and r0 = 0.7 nm is the switching distance. The switching function
  equals 1 when r << r0 (residue is close, fully counted), drops to
  0.5 at r = r0, and decays to 0 when r >> r0. This avoids hard
  cutoff artifacts.

  High WCN = many neighbors = buried active site (harder to access).
  Low WCN = few neighbors = exposed active site (easier to access).

  This is conceptually similar to coordination number in crystallography
  but applied to coarse-grained beads. It complements the SAA metric
  (module 8) by providing a distance-weighted rather than geometric
  measure of burial.

  Units: dimensionless (effective neighbor count)

  Reference: switching function form from Fiorucci & Bhatt,
  Biophys. J. 103, 1840 (2012); also standard in PLUMED.

Figures:
  - wcn_active_sites.{png,svg} : Violin + jitter of WCN per active
    site per construct


7. ACTIVE SITE ANALYSIS
-------------------------

  Focused analysis on the four catalytic active sites: MD1 (ADP-ribose
  hydrolase), MD2 (ADP-ribose reader), MD3 (ADP-ribose binding), and
  ART (H-Y-E catalytic triad). Three metrics per site:

  Radial Position:
    Distance from the active site COM to the whole-chain COM, divided
    by the chain's Rg. Values > 1 mean the site is at or beyond the
    chain's "surface" (peripheral); values < 1 mean the site is buried
    toward the interior. This tells you whether the active site faces
    outward (accessible to binding partners) or inward (occluded by
    the chain).
    Units: dimensionless ratio

  RMSF (Root Mean Square Fluctuation):
    Average positional fluctuation of the catalytic residues over the
    trajectory, after subtracting the mean position. Measures how much
    the active site moves around — high RMSF means the site is in a
    flexible region (which can mean either unfolded or on a mobile loop),
    low RMSF means the site is rigidly held in place.
    Units: nm

  Inter-Site Distances:
    Pairwise distances between active site COMs (MD1-MD2, MD1-MD3,
    MD2-MD3, MD1-ART, etc.). Shows whether catalytic sites cluster
    together or are spread across the structure. Relevant for
    understanding allosteric communication and multi-site coordination.
    Units: nm

Figures:
  - active_site_rmsf.{png,svg}      : Violin + jitter of catalytic RMSF
  - active_site_radial.{png,svg}    : Violin + jitter of radial position
  - intersite_distances.{png,svg}   : Violin + jitter of pairwise inter-
    site distances per construct
  - active_site_summary.{png,svg}   : Heatmap of radial position across
    all constructs and sites


8. STERIC ACCESSIBILITY (SAA, CONE, SHELL)
--------------------------------------------

  Three geometric metrics that assess whether a small molecule or
  binding partner could physically approach each active site, given the
  steric obstruction by the protein's own chain. Computed by ray-casting
  from the active site center of mass.

  Solid Angle Accessibility (SAA):
    Fraction of directions from which the active site can be approached
    without colliding with the protein chain. 200 uniformly distributed
    rays (Fibonacci sphere sampling) are cast outward from the active
    site COM. Each ray is tested against all non-local residues
    (sequence separation > 10) within 5 nm. A ray is "blocked" if any
    residue's CA bead is within 0.5 nm (probe radius) of the ray path.
      SAA = (number of unblocked rays) / (total rays)
    SAA = 1.0 means fully exposed (no obstruction from any direction).
    SAA = 0.0 means completely buried (blocked from every direction).
    SAA > 0.5 is considered readily accessible; SAA < 0.3 is occluded.
    Units: fraction (0 to 1)

  Maximum Approach Cone Angle:
    The half-angle of the widest unobstructed cone centered on the
    active site. Computed from the same ray-casting: the unblocked
    rays are averaged to find the principal open direction, then the
    cone angle is the angle from that direction to the most peripheral
    unblocked ray. A wider cone means there is a large solid angle
    through which a binding partner can approach; a narrow cone means
    access is restricted to a single tight channel.
    Cone > 90 degrees = more than a hemisphere is open.
    Cone < 45 degrees = access is very restricted.
    Units: degrees

  Shell Density:
    Volume fraction of the spherical shell between 2 nm and 5 nm from
    the active site that is occupied by the protein's own chain beads.
    Each CA bead is modeled as a sphere of radius 0.25 nm. Higher shell
    density means more of the surrounding space is filled by chain
    residues, making it harder for external molecules to diffuse in.
      Shell density = (N_beads_in_shell * V_bead) / V_shell
    Typical values are 0.005-0.04 (the shell is mostly empty solvent
    in CG implicit-solvent simulations).
    Units: dimensionless volume fraction

Figures:
  - accessibility_saa.{png,svg}     : Violin + jitter of SAA per site
  - accessibility_cone.{png,svg}    : Violin + jitter of cone angle
  - accessibility_summary.{png,svg} : Three-panel heatmap (SAA, cone,
    shell) across all constructs and sites


ADDITIONAL FIGURES (figure_accessibility.py)
=============================================

Seven publication-quality composite figures generated from the
accessibility data:

  fig1_accessibility_overview : 3-panel heatmap (SAA, cone, shell density)
    with numeric annotations. Quick overview of all metrics x sites x
    constructs.

  fig2_saa_per_site : Four panels (one per active site), each showing
    SAA distributions across constructs as violin + jitter + mean/SD.

  fig3_metrics_by_construct : Three panels (SAA, cone, shell) with all
    four sites grouped within each construct. Violin + jitter + SD bars.
    Answers: "for a given construct, how do the sites compare?"

  fig4_domain_context : Line plots of each metric vs construct size
    (ordered by chain length: MD only -> MKA -> Core -> FL). Shows how
    adding more domains changes accessibility — the "domain context
    effect". Includes individual replicate points and SD bands.

  fig5_docking_feasibility : 2D scatter of cone angle (y) vs SAA (x),
    with each point = one construct x site combination. Quadrants
    labeled "readily accessible" (high SAA, wide cone) vs "sterically
    occluded" (low SAA, narrow cone). Individual replicates shown as
    faded points behind mean +/- SD crosshairs.

  fig6_schematic : Cartoon diagrams explaining what each metric measures
    (ray-casting for SAA, cone geometry, shell density counting).

  fig7_site_ranking : Horizontal bar chart ranking active sites by SAA
    within each construct, with SD error bars.


SIMULATION CONSTRUCTS
======================

  fl     : Full-length PARP14 (1801 residues). All 11 domain units.
  md     : Macrodomains only (586 res). MD1L1 + MD2 + MD3.
  core   : Core domains (1051 res). KH7a + MD1L1 + MD2 + MD3 +
           KHb-KH8 + WWE + ART.
  mka    : MD + KH + ART (999 res). MD1L1 + MD2 + MD3 + KHb-KH8 +
           WWE + ART.
  norrm  : No-RRM (1474 res). KH1-6 + KH7a + MD1L1 + MD2 + MD3 +
           KHb-KH8 + WWE + ART. (FL minus RRM1/2/3)
  noart  : No-ART (1275 res). KH1-6 + KH7a + MD1L1 + MD2 + MD3 +
           KHb-KH8 + WWE. (FL minus RRM1/2/3 and ART)

  Each construct: 25 replicates (5 AF3 seeds x 5 samples), 20 ns each.
  Equilibration: first 0.5 ns discarded. Effective: 25 x 19.5 ns per set.

  Active sites present per construct:
    fl, core, mka, norrm : MD1, MD2, MD3, ART
    md, noart            : MD1, MD2, MD3 (no ART domain)
