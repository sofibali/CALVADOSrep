## The interface

PARP9 KH2 ↔ DTX3L KH4 — which is the published K557–K401 crosslink. Restrained
CALVADOS reaches it in 90–96% of bound frames and HyRes docked in 100%, and
the two force fields get there independently. `analysis/domain_contact_maps.csv`
(`figure_domain_contacts.py`), figures 18–21.

Conditioning matters: an *unconditional* mean minimum distance is meaningless
here, because two unassociated chains in a large box give 15–17 nm for every
domain pair. Read `pct_contact_bound` and `mean_min_bound_nm`, never the
unconditioned columns.

## HyRes does not beat CALVADOS once chain dimension is controlled

CALVADOS expands DTX3L (Rg 3.69 → 6.39 nm) where HyRes holds it at 3.13 nm, so
a raw crosslink-satisfaction comparison rewards HyRes for compaction rather
than for the interface. The decoy-based AUC removes that: rank all 4,752
inter-chain Lys–Lys pairs, ask how the 18 true crosslinks rank among them, with
the 3 restrained pairs excluded. `analysis/decoy_auc_ci.csv`
(`score_decoys.py`, `figure_decoy_control.py`), 2000-sample bootstrap:

| model | AUC | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| CALVADOS unrestrained (docked) | 0.802 | 0.706–0.886 |
| CALVADOS +3 XL harmonic k=20 | 0.795 | 0.704–0.883 |
| CALVADOS +3 XL Gō r=1.5 k=100 | 0.788 | 0.667–0.894 |
| CALVADOS unrestrained (separated) | 0.734 | 0.628–0.831 |
| HyRes matched (docked) | 0.708 | 0.570–0.837 |
| CALVADOS ternary (docked) | 0.646 | 0.526–0.759 |
| HyRes matched (separated) | 0.481 | 0.341–0.619 |
| CALVADOS +21 XL IMProv-tiered | 0.393 | 0.387–0.399 |

**The intervals overlap, so the models cannot be ranked against each other.**
What the table does establish is the negative: HyRes's apparent advantage does
not survive the dimension control.

SASD rescoring (`score_sasd.py`, `analysis/sasd_rescore.csv`) costs every model
12–42% of its satisfied crosslinks and reorders nothing.

## Convergence

The restrained domain maps are converged — max change 2.3 percentage points
over a 6× increase in sampling. The unrestrained map is noise: sd > mean,
change 8.8 pp. `analysis/domain_contacts_conv.csv` (`analyze_convergence.py`).
Per-replicate (`analysis/domain_contacts_perrep.csv`) matters as much as
pooled: the HyRes separated arm's pooled MD1↔RRM 75% is the mean of {0, 99, 84}
and **no** domain pair there is reproducible in 3/3 replicates.

## Known gap: split KH1 restraints are missing

`prepare_hyres_matched.py:98` builds within-block pairs only, so 85 of the
custom restraints that bridge the two halves of PARP9 KH1 were never emitted.
The halves separate to 5.02 nm in HyRes against 0.87 nm in CALVADOS. Any HyRes
conclusion that touches PARP9 KH1 carries this caveat until the runs are
redone. `data/check_split_kh1.py`.

## The 2026-08-18 DMTMM screen is not usable as evidence

`score_xlms_fdr.py` → `analysis/xlms_screen_fdr.csv`, over
`docs/20260818_SB31_P14-7_XLMS/`. Target–decoy FDR (Walzthoeni 2012):

| sample | TT | TD | DD | FDR | TT:TD:DD | decoys in top 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SB31 (3 reps pooled) | 355 | 588 | 298 | **0.82** | 1 : 1.66 : 0.84 | 12 |
| P14-7 +ADPr (3 reps pooled) | 113 | 205 | 102 | **0.91** | 1 : 1.81 : 0.90 | 12 |

Random matching gives 1:2:1, and both samples sit close to it. The score does
not separate — 12 of the 20 best-scoring PSMs are decoys in each sample — and
reproducibility barely helps (target fraction rises 0.286 → 0.328 for SB31,
0.269 → 0.400 for P14-7, i.e. 1.15× and 1.49×). Residue numbering also does not
fit the stated constructs: SB31 sites run 7–1324 against a 1,016-residue
construct. Treat the screen as a screen; do not score a model against it.

Chemistry note for when a usable set arrives: DMTMM is zero-length and couples
Lys to Asp/Glu, not Lys to Lys like BS3, so the Cα–Cα cutoff is ~20 Å, not 30.

## Exposure does not discriminate reactive residues

`figure_crosslinkable_residues.py` → `analysis/crosslinkable_residues.csv`,
figure 23. RSA by freesasa on the all-atom AF3/AF2 input structures the
simulations were seeded from, Chothia normalisation and thresholds imported
from `../sim_analysis/figure_sasa_faces.py` so the two cannot drift.

| chain | Lys exposed | Asp/Glu exposed |
|---|---|---|
| PARP14 | 158/162 (97.5%) | 196/233 (84.1%) |
| PARP9 | 64/66 (97.0%) | 69/91 (75.8%) |
| DTX3L | 69/72 (95.8%) | 91/97 (93.8%) |

96–98% of lysines are surface in all three chains, so a reactivity filter keyed
on burial removes essentially nothing from either the BS3 or the DMTMM
candidate space. Acidic residues are buried more often — the expected signature
of buried salt bridges — but still mostly reactive. This is one static
structure and a CG trajectory cannot refine it, so it is a structural prior,
not a reactivity measurement.
